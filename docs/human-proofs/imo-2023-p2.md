# Golden: IMO 2023 Problem 2 (shortest proof)

> Let ABC be an acute-angled triangle with AB < AC. Let Ω be the circumcircle of ABC. Let S be the midpoint of the arc CB of Ω containing A. The perpendicular from A to BC meets BS at D and meets Ω again at E ≠ A. The line through D parallel to BC meets line BE at L. Denote the circumcircle of triangle BDL by ω. Let ω meet Ω again at P ≠ B. Prove that the line tangent to ω at P meets line BS on the internal angle bisector of ∠BAC.

Program: `imo2023p2.geo` (repo root).
- N is the second meet of AI with Ω (the midpoint of arc BC not containing A).
- S = NO ∩ Ω.
- X is the point of BS with XP ⟂ PO₁, where O₁ is the centre of ω.
- The goal is `eqangle(A, B, A, X, A, X, A, C)`.

Command: `agstudio best imo2023p2.geo --budget 60`. Shortest proof: **51 steps, 2 auxiliary points, 6268 distinct proofs examined**:
- `x1 = intersect(perp(O, BE), BS)`, called **T** below;
- `L' = parallelogram(A, L, B)`, which is never cited and so dropped from the setup (§6.7).

Replica for the prototype: `imo2023p2.geo` plus `T = meet(perp_line(O, line(B, E)), line(B, S))` and `Q = parallelogram(A, L, B)`. DDAR proves it directly with the same derived facts. The replica prints 53 steps, because the helper `_11` of `perp_line` adds two restatements; its steps 037–053 are the best proof's 035–051. Step numbers below are the **best proof's**.

## Target human proof — EN (exact)

> *∡ denotes directed angles modulo 180°.*
>
> Ω has centre O, and N, S, E, P lie on Ω with NS a diameter. The circle ω = (BDL) has centre O₁, and XP is tangent to ω at P (XP ⟂ PO₁).
> Let T be the point of BS with OT ⟂ BE.
>
> **Claim 1.** T, E, S, O are concyclic.
>
> *Proof.* ∡STO = ∡SBE + 90° = ∡SNE + 90° = ∡NEO + 90° = ∡SEO
> (OT ⟂ BE; inscribed angles in Ω; ON = OE; ∠NES = 90° as NS is a diameter).
>
> **Claim 2.** T, P, X, O are concyclic.
>
> *Proof.* Angle chasing with Claim 1, the tangency of XP to ω, the inscribed angles in Ω and in ω, DL ∥ BC, OT ⟂ BE, AN bisecting ∠BAC, ∠NBS = ∠NPS = 90° and OE = OP = OS gives ∡SXP = ∡TOP.
>
> **Claim 3.** T, P, E, D are concyclic.
>
> *Proof.* Angle chasing with Claim 1, the inscribed angles in Ω and in ω, DL ∥ BC, OT ⟂ BE, AN bisecting ∠BAC and ∠NBS = ∠NPS = 90° gives ∡ETD = ∡EPD.
>
> **Claim 4.** XA = XP.
>
> *Proof.* OA = OP, OX is common, and ∡AOX = ∡XOP: angle chasing with Claims 2 and 3, the tangency of XP to ω, the inscribed angles in Ω and in ω, AN bisecting ∠BAC, OT ⟂ BE, DL ∥ BC, ∠NBS = 90° and OB = OP. Hence △OAX ≅ △OPX.
>
> Finally, as XA = XP, angle chasing with the tangency of XP to ω, the inscribed angles in Ω and in ω, DL ∥ BC, AD ⟂ BC, AN bisecting ∠BAC and ∠NCS = 90° gives ∡BAX = ∡XAC, so X lies on the bisector of ∠BAC. ∎

Claim 4, XA = XP, is the "Main claim" of Evan Chen's solution (`IMO-2023-notes.pdf`: "We have XP = XA"). The writer reaches it because the goal's cheapest certificate uses the atom `Isosceles(X; A, P)`, read from the ratio rows of 051, rather than the angle rows of the congruence.

## Hand checks of the Claim 1 chain (directed angles, lines up to the proven collinearities)

1. ∡STO = ∡SBE + 90°. With T on line BS, ∡STO − ∡SBE = dir(TO) − dir(BE) = 90°, since OT ⟂ BE.
2. ∡SBE = ∡SNE: inscribed angles on chord SE of Ω.
3. ∡SNE = ∡ONE, as N, O, S are collinear; and ∡ONE = ∡NEO because ON = OE.
4. ∡NEO + 90° = ∡SEO, since EN ⟂ ES (Thales on the diameter NS).

Then ∡STO = ∡SEO, so T, E, S, O are concyclic. Every link is an admissible atom (§6.2), so stage 1 (BFS) finds it. The prototype has no stage 1; its certificate takes the longer path through A (OA = OS, OA = OE).
