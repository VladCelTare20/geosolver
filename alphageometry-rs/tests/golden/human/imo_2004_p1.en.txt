Angles written ∠ are read from the figure.
∡(a, b) is the angle through which line a must be turned counter-clockwise to become parallel to b, taken modulo 180°; ∡XYZ means ∡(YX, YZ). These angles do not depend on how the figure is drawn.
Directed angles are needed only in step 8, where the figure-angle form would depend on the configuration.
Let ω₁ be the circle through B, M, R, P.
Let ω₂ be the circle through C, N, R, P.
Let F be the foot of the perpendicular from B to AR.

Plan: it suffices to show that NC ∥ OF, R, N, F, O are concyclic and R, A, F, P are collinear.

1. BC is a diameter, so ∠BNC = 90°. (1)
2. We show that F, N, B, A are concyclic:
    ∠BFA = 90°     [FB ⟂ AR]
         = ∠BNA    [∠BNC = 90°, by (1)]
So F, N, B, A are concyclic. (2)
3. AR bisects ∠BAC, so ∠FAB = ∠CAF. F, N, B, A concyclic, by (2), so FB = NF (equal or supplementary inscribed angles stand on equal chords). (3)
4. OB = ON and, by (3), FB = FN, so OF is the perpendicular bisector of BN; hence OF ⟂ BN. (4)
5. NC ⟂ NB, by (1) and OF ⟂ NB, by (4), so NC ∥ OF. (5)
6. We show that R, N, F, O are concyclic:
    ∠NFA = ∠NBM      [F, N, B, A concyclic, by (2)]
         = ½·∠NOM    [central angle ∠MON = 2∠MBN]
         = ∠NOR      [OR bisects ∠MON]
So R, N, F, O are concyclic. (6)
7. AB·AM = AC·AN (power of the point A with respect to the circle (BMCN)). A has the same power with respect to the circles ω₁ and ω₂, so A lies on their radical axis, the line PR; hence R, A, F, P are collinear. (7)
8. We show that P lies on the line BC:
    ∡PCB = ∡PCN + ∡NCO    [angle addition]
         = ∡PCN + ∡ONC    [OC = ON: triangle OCN is isosceles]
         = ∡ONC + ∡PRN    [inscribed angles in ω₂]
         = ∡NOF + ∡PRN    [NC ∥ OF, by (5)]
         = ∡NRF + ∡PRN    [R, N, F, O concyclic, by (6)]
         = ∡PRF           [angle addition]
         = 0°             [R, A, F, P collinear, by (7)]
So P lies on the line BC, as required. ∎
