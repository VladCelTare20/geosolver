∡(a, b) is the directed angle between the lines a and b: the angle through which line a must be turned counter-clockwise to become parallel to b, taken modulo 180°; ∡XYZ means ∡(YX, YZ). These are angles between lines, not ordinary angles: 0° and 180° are the same directed angle, so their equalities are written ≡ … (mod 180°).
They are used only in step 10, where no derivation with ordinary angles was found.
Let Ω be the circumcircle of triangle ABC, with centre O; N, S, E, P lie on Ω.
Let ω₁ be the circle centred at O₁ through B, D, L, P.

Plan: it suffices to show that A, I, X are collinear.

1. NS is a diameter, so ∠NES = 90°. (1)
2. We show that NB = NC:
    ∠NBC = ∠NAC    [inscribed angles in Ω]
         = ∠BAN    [AI bisects ∠BAC]
         = ∠BCN    [inscribed angles in Ω]
Hence NB = NC. (2)
3. OB = OC and, by (2), NB = NC, so ON is the perpendicular bisector of BC; hence ON ⟂ BC. (3)
4. AD ⟂ BC and ON ⟂ BC, by (3), so AD ∥ ON. (4)
5. We show that T, E, S, O are concyclic:
    ∠BTO = 90° − ∠SBE    [OT ⟂ BE]
         = 90° − ∠SNE    [inscribed angles in Ω]
         = ∠OSE          [∠NES = 90°, by (1)]
         = ∠SEO          [OS = OE: triangle OSE is isosceles]
∠STO = ∠SEO, S lies on the segment TB and T, E lie on the same side of SO, so by the converse of the inscribed angle theorem, T, E, S, O are concyclic. (5)
6. We show that ∠OTX = ∠OEB − ∠BDL + 180°:
    ∠OTX = 180° − ∠OES           [OSET cyclic, by (5)]
         = ∠OEN + 90°            [∠NES = 90°, by (1)]
         = ∠BEN + ∠OEB + 90°     [angle addition]
         = ∠BSO + ∠OEB + 90°     [inscribed angles in Ω]
         = ∠OEB − ∠DBC + 180°    [ON ⟂ BC, by (3)]
         = ∠OEB − ∠BDL + 180°    [DL ∥ BC; alternate angles] (6)
7. We show that T, P, X, O are concyclic:
    ∠OTX = ∠OEB − ∠BDL + 180°           [by (6)]
         = ∠OEB − ∠BPL + 180°           [inscribed angles in ω₁]
         = ∠EBO − ∠BPL + 180°           [OE = OB: triangle OEB is isosceles]
         = ∠EBO + ∠BPO − ∠OPL + 180°    [angle addition]
         = ∠EBO + ∠OBP − ∠OPL + 180°    [OP = OB: triangle OPB is isosceles]
         = ∠EBP − ∠OPL + 180°           [angle addition]
         = ∠OPX                         [PX is tangent to ω₁ (tangent–chord angle)]
∠OTX = ∠OPX and T, P lie on the same side of OX, so by the converse of the inscribed angle theorem, T, P, X, O are concyclic. (7)
8. We show that T, P, E, D are concyclic:
    ∠ETD = ∠EOS                  [exterior angle of the cyclic quadrilateral TEOS, by (5)]
         = 180° − ∠OEA           [AD ∥ ON, by (4)]
         = ½·∠EOA + 90°          [OA = OE: triangle OAE is isosceles]
         = ∠EPA + 90°            [central angle ∠AOE = 2∠APE]
         = ∠DAP − ∠PED + 90°     [exterior angle of △APE]
         = ∠EBP − ∠PED + 90°     [exterior angle of the cyclic quadrilateral AEBP]
         = ∠EBC − ∠EPB + 180°    [AD ⟂ BC]
         = ∠BLD − ∠EPB + 180°    [DL ∥ BC; corresponding angles]
         = ∠EPD                  [inscribed angles in ω₁]
∠ETD = ∠EPD and T, P lie on the same side of ED, so by the converse of the inscribed angle theorem, T, P, E, D are concyclic. (8)
9. We show that XA = XP:
    ∠AOX = ∠AOP − ∠XOP    [angle addition]
         = ∠AOP − ∠PTB    [exterior angle of the cyclic quadrilateral TPOX, by (7)]
         = ∠AOP − ∠AEP    [exterior angle of the cyclic quadrilateral TDEP, by (8)]
         = ∠AEP           [central angle ∠AOP = 2∠AEP]
         = ∠BTP           [exterior angle of the cyclic quadrilateral TDEP, by (8)]
         = ∠XOP           [exterior angle of the cyclic quadrilateral TXOP, by (7)]
OA = OP, so △OAX ≅ △OPX (SAS). Hence XA = XP. (9)
10. We show that A, I, X are collinear:
    ∡IAX ≡ ∡SAX + 90°                 [NS is a diameter]
         ≡ ∡SAX + ∡ADL + ∡(DL, BC)    [AD ⟂ BC]
         ≡ ∡SAX + ∡ADL                [DL ∥ BC]
         ≡ ∡PAX + ∡SAP + ∡ADL         [angle addition]
         ≡ ∡PAX + ∡XBP + ∡ADL         [inscribed angles in Ω]
         ≡ ∡PAX + ∡DPX + ∡ADL         [PX is tangent to ω₁ (tangent–chord angle)]
         ≡ ∡DPX + ∡XPA + ∡ADL         [XA = XP: triangle XAP is isosceles, by (9)]
         ≡ ∡DPA + ∡ADL                [angle addition]
         ≡ ∡BPA + ∡DPB + ∡ADL         [angle addition]
         ≡ ∡DPB + ∡BEA + ∡ADL         [inscribed angles in Ω]
         ≡ ∡BEA + ∡DLB + ∡ADL         [inscribed angles in ω₁]
         ≡ 0° (mod 180°)              [angle sum in △DEL]
So A, I, X are collinear. (10)
11. A, I, X collinear, by (10) and AI bisects ∠BAC, so ∡BAX ≡ ∡XAC (mod 180°), as required. ∎
